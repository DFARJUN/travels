Three loops out of New York, and back to it

Same airport in, same airport out. Three nights in the city, eight on the road. One loop goes north into village New England, one south down the Blue Ridge, one west to Niagara and Chicago.

Recommendation

Take Loop A, New England.

Closing the loop is what separates these three. Loop A goes up the inland side and comes home down the coast, so no day is longer than 2h30 and you never drive the same road twice. Loop B has to turn around and retrace the same ridge. Loop C only works if you fly one leg.

Rationale
1,405 km with a 2h30 ceiling. Eight nights, seven different towns, peak colour 5 to 15 October.
Cost
No Niagara, no Chicago. Loop C is the only one that gets you those, and it costs a 6-hour driving day plus a flight.
Next action
Book Woodstock and Stowe this week. They sell out 4 to 6 months ahead for October.

What only this one gives you The densest run of small towns in the country, and a loop where you never drive the same road twice. Mountains on the way up, fishing harbours on the way back. 750 km of road built for no purpose except to be beautiful. No trucks, no billboards, no junctions, 45 mph. Nothing else in America is like it. The two icons. Standing under Niagara on the boat, and Chicago, which is the best big city between the coasts.
Choose it if You want the most villages and the least driving, and you care that October looks the way October is supposed to look. The drive itself is the holiday, and you would rather have one great road than seven pretty towns. You would regret missing Niagara and Chicago more than you would enjoy another covered bridge.
Skip it if You want a famous landmark to point at. There isn't one on this loop, and that is deliberate. You need a town at the end of each day with something going on. Most of these have 7,000 people and one street. Long driving days ruin trips for you, or you did not come to America to spend three nights in a metropolis.

The loops in full

Loop A. Up the mountains, home along the coast

The cleanest loop of the three. North through the Berkshires into Vermont, east across the White Mountains to Maine, then south down the coast through fishing villages and back into the city. Seven towns, no road driven twice, nothing over 2h30.

1,405 km17h wheel time8 nights out2h30 longest day

00

New York

2 nights now, 1 at the end
Arrival. Do not rent the car yet.

You have done this city, so treat the front two nights as a jet lag buffer rather than a second first visit. Base in the East Village or Lower East Side and stay on foot. Collect the car the morning you leave.

  • Russ & Daughters for bagels, then the Lower East Side and Nolita
  • West Village side streets, the High Line north into Chelsea Market
  • Manhattan parking runs 50 to 70 dollars a night. A loop means you return the car to the same branch and pay no drop fee at all
01

The Berkshires

1 night
220 km / 2h30 / Taconic Parkway

Your first small town, the gentle version. Take the Taconic rather than the interstate. It is the pretty road and it costs you nothing.

  • Stockbridge Main Street, the one Norman Rockwell painted. Ten minutes, one photo, move on
  • Great Barrington for the night: bookshops, Rubiner's cheesemongers, sourdough pizza at Baba Louie's
  • Skip MASS MoCA. Famous, and a three hour art warehouse
02

Woodstock, Vermont

2 nights
210 km / 2h30 / Route 7 through Manchester

The one people call the prettiest town in America, and it earns it. Village green, covered bridge in the middle of town, everything within twenty minutes. Your one proper slow day.

  • Sugarbush Farm up a dirt road: free cheese and maple tasting, they let you try everything
  • Quechee Gorge and the Simon Pearce glassblowing studio inside a working mill
  • Mount Tom if you want one short climb: 45 minutes up, valley view, done
03

Stowe, Vermont

1 night
130 km / 1h30 / Route 100. Northernmost point of the loop

Mountain Vermont. Small main street, big peaks behind it, and the best golden hour of the trip.

  • Cold Hollow Cider Mill in Waterbury: apple cider donuts made in front of you, still warm
  • Smugglers' Notch, a road that squeezes between cliffs and boulders
  • Trapp Family Lodge at sunset, yes that family. Austrian bakery, beer hall, mountain lawn
04

White Mountains

1 night
175 km / 2h15 / base in North Conway

The dramatic day. The road is the attraction, the town is just where you sleep.

  • Kancamagus Highway, 55 km of forest and overlooks with nothing built on it
  • The Flume Gorge, a boardwalk through a narrow rock chasm, about an hour
  • Mount Washington by cog railway or auto road, if the weather holds
05

Portland, Maine

1 night
140 km / 1h45 / the turn for home

The food stop, and the point where the loop bends south. Small city, cobblestone Old Port, a restaurant scene well out of proportion to its size. One night is tight here, so eat early and eat twice.

  • Eventide Oyster Co. brown butter lobster roll, and Duckfat for duck fat fries
  • Tandem Coffee + Bakery, and The Holy Donut, made with potato
  • Portland Head Light at Cape Elizabeth, the most photographed lighthouse in America
06

Rockport and Cape Ann

1 night
170 km / 2h / down the coast, stopping in Portsmouth

A granite fishing village on a hook of land north of Boston. This is the coastal half of the loop paying off: harbours, lobster boats, and no mountains in sight.

  • Bearskin Neck, a narrow spit of old fishing shacks turned into shops, and Motif No. 1, the red shack at the end of it
  • Roy Moore Lobster Co., eat it on the back deck over the water, paper plates only
  • Stop in Portsmouth, New Hampshire for lunch on the way down. Proper harbour town, Market Square
07

Mystic, Connecticut

1 night
160 km / 2h, then 2h30 back into New York

Last night on the road, and a good one. A drawbridge in the middle of a seafaring village, and it is 2h30 from returning the car.

  • Mystic Seaport, a working 19th century shipyard with tall ships you can board
  • The bascule drawbridge lifts on the hour through the season. Everyone stops to watch
  • Stonington Borough next door is prettier and half as busy. Go there for dinner instead

Loop B. Down the Blue Ridge and back up the Piedmont

Two purpose-built scenic roads end to end, Skyline Drive into the Blue Ridge Parkway, then a turnaround and a different way home through Jefferson country and Gettysburg. Mountain towns rather than pretty villages.

1,550 km19h30 wheel time8 nights out4h longest day

00

New York

2 nights now, 1 at the end
Arrival. Same as Loop A.

Land, sleep it off, one real day on foot, then out. Car collected on departure morning, returned to the same branch, no drop fee.

01

Lancaster, Pennsylvania

1 night
260 km / 3h / the weak leg, mostly interstate

Pennsylvania Dutch country. Horse and buggy on the shoulder, farm stands, a genuinely different America. Worth the dull drive to get here.

  • Central Market in Lancaster, the oldest continuously running farmers market in the country
  • Bird-in-Hand and Intercourse, the Amish farm villages. Yes, those are the real names
  • Whoopie pies and shoofly pie from a roadside stand, cash only
02

Shenandoah, Virginia

2 nights
240 km / 3h / base in Luray or Sperryville

Skyline Drive runs 170 km along the ridge with 75 overlooks. You do not hike it, you drive it slowly and stop constantly.

  • Skyline Drive end to end, a full unhurried day with stops
  • Luray Caverns, a cathedral-sized cave with an organ that plays the stalactites. Odd and great
  • Sperryville and Washington VA, tiny mountain towns with good coffee
03

Lexington, Virginia

1 night
160 km / 2h / where Skyline Drive becomes the Parkway

A town of 7,000 people, all red brick and white columns, wedged between two old colleges. Nothing to see, which is the point.

  • Walk Main Street end to end in twenty minutes, then do it again
  • Devils Backbone brewery out in the fields, if the weather is good
  • Natural Bridge, a 65 metre limestone arch Jefferson once owned
04

Blue Ridge Parkway, Roanoke and Floyd

2 nights
130 km / 2h on the Parkway. Turnaround point of the loop

The southernmost point you can reach and still get home. Base in Roanoke for the food or Floyd for the strangeness.

  • Mabry Mill, the single most photographed spot on the entire Parkway
  • Floyd Country Store Friday Night Jamboree: old-time Appalachian music, locals flatfoot dancing, no alcohol, no irony. The real thing
  • Roanoke City Market, running since 1882, and Peaks of Otter for sunset
05

Charlottesville, Virginia

1 night
190 km / 2h30 / heading home on the eastern side

College town at the foot of the mountains with a genuinely good pedestrian main street. October here is apple season.

  • Monticello, Jefferson's house. A UNESCO site and worth the 90 minutes
  • Downtown Mall, eight blocks of brick with no cars on it
  • Carter Mountain Orchard: apple picking, cider donuts and a view over the whole valley
06

Gettysburg, Pennsylvania

1 night
250 km / 3h, then 4h back into New York

Last night out, and the longest drive of the trip waiting the next morning. The town itself is small and barely changed since the 1860s.

  • Drive the battlefield auto tour yourself, about three hours, 16 stops, no guide needed
  • Little Round Top at sunset for the view over the whole field
  • Leave early the next day. The final leg is 4 hours and the last of it is New York traffic

Loop C. Niagara and Chicago, home by air

The bucket-list route. Driving both ways would add twelve hours and two dead days, so this closes the loop with a two-hour flight instead: drive out, drop the car in Chicago, fly back for your last night in New York.

1,810 km driving18h wheel time8 nights out6h longest day

00

New York

2 nights now, 1 at the end
Arrival, and the last night after you fly back from Chicago.

Unlike the other two loops, this one has a one-way car rental. Budget an extra 150 to 400 dollars for the Chicago drop, plus roughly 100 to 150 each for the flight home.

01

Finger Lakes, New York

1 night
430 km / 4h30 / the longest opening leg of the three loops

Long thin glacial lakes with vineyards down both sides and gorges cut into the rock between them. Base in Watkins Glen or Ithaca.

  • Watkins Glen State Park, a 2 km gorge trail past 19 waterfalls on stone staircases. The best short walk anywhere in this document
  • Taughannock Falls, taller than Niagara, ten minutes from the car park
  • Seneca Lake wineries, and Ithaca for coffee and a college town evening
02

Niagara Falls

2 nights
230 km / 2h30

Worth every bit of the hype from up close, and the American town beside it is grim. Stay on the Canadian side for the panorama, or in Buffalo for the food.

  • Maid of the Mist boat into the base of the falls, running to late October
  • Cave of the Winds, a wooden walkway directly under Bridal Veil Falls. You get soaked
  • Buffalo is 30 minutes away: the original Anchor Bar wings, and the Elmwood Village strip
03

Cleveland, Ohio

1 night
320 km / 3h30 / along the Lake Erie shore

Rust belt city halfway through a comeback. One night is exactly right.

  • West Side Market, a 1912 food hall that is still a real market, not a tourist one
  • Rock and Roll Hall of Fame. The one museum in this whole document worth two hours
  • Cuyahoga Valley on the way out: Brandywine Falls is a 20 minute boardwalk loop
04

Amish Country, Ohio

1 night
130 km / 1h30 / base in Berlin or Millersburg

Holmes County holds the largest Amish settlement in the world, bigger than Lancaster and far less touristed. Horse and buggy traffic, no power lines, a different century.

  • Heini's Cheese Chalet for the tasting counter, Der Dutchman for pie
  • Drive the back roads between Charm, Walnut Creek and Winesburg
  • Much of it closes Sunday, and photographing people is not welcome. Buy from the farm stands instead
05

Chicago

3 nights
700 km / 6h, stopping at Indiana Dunes. Then fly home to New York

Break the drive at Indiana Dunes, sand dunes on Lake Michigan with the Chicago skyline floating across the water. Then three nights in the best walkable big city in the middle of the country. Drop the car the moment you arrive.

  • Architecture river cruise. The one organised tour worth taking anywhere in this document
  • Millennium Park and the Bean, the Riverwalk, and 360 Chicago for the tilting glass ledge
  • Deep dish at Lou Malnati's, Italian beef at Al's, a hot dog at Portillo's
  • Wicker Park and Logan Square for neighbourhood wandering, Green Mill for late jazz

Applies to all three

Book accommodation now

Village inns in Vermont and New Hampshire sell out four to six months ahead for October. The other two loops are more forgiving, but Niagara and Charlottesville still fill. Whichever loop you pick, lock the two-night stops first.

Avoid Columbus Day weekend, 10 to 12 October 2026

The busiest weekend of the year on all three loops. If your dates overlap it, be parked in one town for those three nights rather than driving between them.

The loop pays for itself on the car

Loops A and B return the car to the branch you took it from, so there is no one-way drop fee at all. That is worth 150 to 400 dollars against the linear version. Loop C is the exception because you leave the car in Chicago.

The car

Israeli licence plus an International Driving Permit, sorted before you fly. Take E-ZPass from the rental desk for northeastern tolls. Collect the car on the morning you leave New York, never for the city nights.

Paperwork

ESTA for the US, apply at least 72 hours out. Loop C only: if you want the Canadian side of Niagara, you also need a Canadian eTA. Cheap and online, but you cannot get it at the border.

Where to sleep

Airbnb works in New York, Portland, Chicago and Charlottesville. In the small towns book a village inn instead. Same price, better located, breakfast included, and in a village of 200 people that is a large part of the experience.

Timing the colour

Foliage runs north to south and high ground down. Loop A peaks 5 to 15 October, Loop C around 10 to 20 October, Loop B at elevation 15 to 25 October. All three are sequenced to follow the colour rather than chase it.
